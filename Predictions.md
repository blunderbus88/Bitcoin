# Predictions

These predictions preserve a timestamped record of concerns held at the founding of this chain. They are not Principles, nor claims of certainty. They are expectations about where BTC-SHA256 may go and the pressures we believe it will face.

Each prediction is preserved so future readers can judge for themselves where we were right, where we were wrong, and whether the concerns that brought us here were justified.

## 1. Frozen Satoshi-era coins.

BTC-SHA256 will adopt or seriously pursue consensus rules that make some dormant or vulnerable coins unspendable under their original conditions. This is already a live controversy through BIP-361 and related quantum proposals.

## 2. Uncapping of 21 million

As subsidy declines, the “security budget” will increasingly be used to argue for perpetual issuance, tail emission, or another mechanism that increases effective supply.

## 3. Demurrage

Rather than openly abandoning the nominal 21 million ceiling, a proposal will emerge to reduce the value of old or dormant UTXOs and redirect that value toward miner security budget.

## 4. Conditional self-custody

The quantum debate will establish a precedent that coins validly owned under existing rules can become unspendable unless their owners take prescribed action within a specified window. BIP-361 already proposes sunsetting legacy signature methods, so this is partly underway.

## 5. BTC-SHA256 will continue moving toward institutional and industrial infrastructure.

Mining and protocol infrastructure will increasingly involve large pools and corporations, even while technologies such as Stratum V2 attempt to return transaction selection to individual miners. The current Stratum V2 effort illustrates both sides: major pools representing a large share of hashrate are participating specifically in an effort to decentralize block construction.

## 6. Node runners will face legal challenges over on-chain content.

In some jurisdictions, node runners will face explicit legal or regulatory challenges because their nodes store or relay illegal content embedded in the blockchain or propagated through their mempools. As arbitrary data use expands, the assumption that neutral protocol participation shields ordinary node operators from liability will be tested.

## 7. Running a fully validating node will become increasingly inaccessible to ordinary users.

Continued growth in blockchain and UTXO-set resource requirements, driven in part by data inscriptions and other non-monetary uses of block space, will increase the cost of independently running a fully validating BTC-SHA256 node. Over time, fewer ordinary users will find it practical to maintain one themselves, further concentrating verification in professional and institutional infrastructure.
